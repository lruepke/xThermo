    Phase diagrams¶

    The library computes the exact geometry of the phase regions of H₂O–NaCl along an isobar or an isotherm:

    Method Section Coordinates
    sw.Slice_constP(p) isobaric, at pressure p [Pa] T–X and H–X
    sw.Slice_constT(T) isothermal, at temperature T [K] P–X

    Each result holds the polygons of the multiphase regions (V+L, V+H, L+H, V+L+H), their boundary lines and the invariant points, with names and colours. The plotting helpers draw them with Matplotlib (pip install ".[plotting]"). Unshaded areas are single-phase fluid. The axes are labelled in °C, bar, wt% NaCl and MJ/kg.

    Because these diagrams draw the computed boundaries, they are exact at any resolution. A grid of phase codes, by contrast, has staircase edges, and contour plots of it interpolate between categories that have no order. Use plot_phases (Property maps) only where no section is available, for example for pure water.

    A complete isobaric figure: plot_Pslice¶

    xthermo.plot_Pslice(P0, x0_log_linear=0.01, sw=None) draws temperature (a) and specific enthalpy (b) against salinity at pressure P0. The salinity axis is logarithmic below x0_log_linear (kg/kg; 1 wt% by default) and linear above, with a strip for pure water (X = 0) on the left.

    import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import xthermo
from xthermo import H2ONaCl

sw = H2ONaCl.cH2ONaCl("IAPS84")
r = xthermo.plot_Pslice(300e5, sw=sw)        # isobaric section at 300 bar: T-X (a) and H-X (b)
plt.show()

    At 300 bar, above the critical pressure of water, the vapour + liquid region (pink) begins at the critical point of the mixture at about 408 °C. The vapour + halite region (grey) lies between the two temperatures of the three-phase assemblage V+L+H (484 and 692 °C), and liquid + halite (blue) bounds the salt-rich side. In the enthalpy section (b), V+L+H is not a line but a region (yellow, orange), because the phase proportions change at constant temperature.

    plot_Pslice returns a dictionary: fig, data (the Slice_constP result), ax_T and ax_H (the log and linear halves of each panel), ax_water and handles (for a legend of your own).

    An isothermal figure: plot_Tslice¶

    xthermo.plot_Tslice(T0, x0_log_linear=0.01, sw=None, plog=True) draws pressure against salinity at temperature T0, with a logarithmic pressure axis by default.

    import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import xthermo
from xthermo import H2ONaCl

sw = H2ONaCl.cH2ONaCl("IAPS84")
r = xthermo.plot_Tslice(450 + 273.15, sw=sw)   # isothermal section at 450 °C: P-X
plt.show()

    Your own axes: plot_slice¶

    xthermo.plot_slice(ax, section, x="X", y=None, regions=True, lines=True, points=True, legend=False) draws a section on Matplotlib axes you create, so you can combine it with other plots. x and y are "T", "P", "X" or "H"; y defaults to "T" for an isobaric and "P" for an isothermal section. ax can also be a list of axes. It returns {label: artist} for a legend.

    import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import xthermo
from xthermo import H2ONaCl

sw = H2ONaCl.cH2ONaCl("IAPS84")
section = sw.Slice_constP(25e5)                  # the phase-region geometry at 25 bar

fig, (ax_t, ax_h) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4.2), layout="constrained")
for ax in (ax_t, ax_h):
    ax.set_xscale("log")                         # salinity on a log axis
    ax.set_xlim(1e-4, 100)                       # wt% NaCl
handles = xthermo.plot_slice(ax_t, section, x="X", y="T")
xthermo.plot_slice(ax_h, section, x="X", y="H")
ax_t.set_ylim(0, 1000)
fig.legend(handles.values(), handles.keys(), loc="outside lower center", ncol=6, fontsize="small")
plt.show()

    At 25 bar, below the critical pressure of water, there are two three-phase temperatures (242 °C and 794 °C), and vapour + halite covers most of the diagram between them.

    Set the scale of the axes (ax.set_xscale("log")) before or after calling plot_slice; the polygons are drawn in data coordinates. Pure water, X = 0, cannot be shown on a log axis: plot_Pslice adds the strip for it.

    Known limitations of the section geometry

    The section geometry is checked against the equation of state point by point in the test suite, and agrees away from the boundaries. Three details of the library's geometry are known to be inaccurate: in isobaric H–X sections below about 221 bar a thin sliver of the vapour + liquid region at salinities below 0.13 wt% NaCl is missing; in isothermal sections the liquid + halite region continues below the three-phase pressure, where vapour + halite is stable (the plotting helpers draw vapour + halite on top, so the figure is correct); and isothermal sections below 374 °C contain a spurious "critical point" at X = 0. They are listed with the other known defects.

    Axis helpers¶

    plot_Pslice and plot_Tslice are built from two helpers you can also use directly:

    • xthermo.makeLogLinearAxes(ax, width_logaxis=0.4, xlim_log=(2e-3, 1), xmax=100, ...) replaces ax by a salinity axis that is logarithmic up to xlim_log[1] wt% and linear above. It returns (ax_log, ax_linear); draw on both, e.g. plot_slice([ax_log, ax_linear], section).
    • xthermo.createWaterAxes(ax_log) adds the narrow strip for pure water at the left of the log axis.
