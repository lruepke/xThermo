    Model, verification and limitations¶

    xThermo computes the properties of H₂O–NaCl fluids with the correlations of Driesner & Heinrich (2007) and Driesner (2007), combined with an equation of state for pure water. This page explains the model, what has been verified and what that verification means, where xThermo deliberately differs from other implementations, how the properties of multiphase states are defined, and which limitations are known.

    In brief¶

    • The model is a set of empirical correlations fitted to experimental data from 0 to 1000 °C, 1 to 5000 bar and 0 to 1 mole fraction NaCl. Densities and enthalpies of brines are obtained from those of pure water at a scaled temperature. The correlations were fitted with the IAPS-84 equation of state for water, xThermo's reference backend.
    • Verification shows that xThermo reproduces the model author's published tables and equations, apart from documented differences. It does not show that the model is accurate: that rests on the authors' comparison with experiments.
    • Three deliberate differences from the author's own program concern hot, saline liquids at low pressure (enthalpy, up to about 3 %), vapour compositions above 800.7 °C (up to about 24 %) and the heat capacity of saline phases (by a factor 1/(1 + x)). xThermo follows the published equations and keeps heat capacity consistent with enthalpy.
    • Bulk properties of multiphase states are defined below. The bulk heat capacity contains no latent heat, and the bulk viscosity is not defined; use the phase properties.
    • Known limitations include extrapolated regions, a few confirmed numerical defects with small or local effects, and open questions near the melting point of pure NaCl.

    The model¶

    Phase relations¶

    Driesner & Heinrich (2007) give correlations in temperature, pressure and NaCl mole fraction for every phase boundary of the system: the melting, sublimation and boiling curves of NaCl, the critical curve, the halite liquidus, the three-phase surface vapour + liquid + halite (V+L+H), the compositions of halite-saturated vapour, and the liquid and vapour compositions of the vapour + liquid (V+L) region. Together they define which phases are present at a given temperature, pressure and bulk composition. The Gallery shows the resulting phase diagram at four pressures and in three dimensions.

    Volumetric and thermal properties¶

    Driesner (2007) gives the molar volume, specific enthalpy and isobaric heat capacity of the liquid and vapour phases with a scaled temperature (corresponding-temperature) method. A brine at temperature T, pressure p and composition x is given the molar volume of pure water at the same pressure and a scaled temperature T*_V(T, p, x). In the same way its specific enthalpy is that of pure water at a second scaled temperature T*_h, and the heat capacity is the temperature derivative of that enthalpy. Pure-water properties come from the water equation of state. Where the scaled temperature falls outside the range in which water properties can be used, the volume correlation is extrapolated: at low pressure near boiling, and above about 600 °C below 390 bar. Halite has its own correlations for density and enthalpy.

    Viscosity¶

    The viscosity of brines follows Klyukin et al. (2017), which also evaluates the viscosity of water at a transformed temperature. Water viscosity comes from the IAPWS 2008 formulation with the IAPWS-95 backend and from the NBS/NRC Steam Tables correlation (as implemented in PROST) with the IAPS-84 backend.

    Water backends¶

    Backend Formulation Range of the pure-water class
    "IAPS84" (default of the command-line tool) IAPS-84 (Haar, Gallagher & Kell 1984; Kestin & Sengers 1986), computed with PROST 4.1 260–2500 K, up to 30 000 bar
    "IAPWS95" IAPWS-95 (Wagner & Pruß 2002) 273.165–2273.15 K, up to 5000 bar
    • The Driesner correlations were fitted with IAPS-84. "IAPS84" is therefore the reference backend; results with "IAPWS95" are a variant of the model, not a separate validation of it.
    • Most phase-boundary correlations do not depend on the backend, for example the V+L+H pressure, and the critical curve above 500 °C.
    • Below 500 °C the critical curve uses the critical point of the selected water backend. With "IAPWS95" the critical pressure is up to about 0.07 % higher, and the curve has a step of about 0.03 % at 500 °C.
    • V+L vapour compositions differ between the backends by about 1 % between 300 and 450 °C, and by more at lower temperatures and close to the boiling pressure of water, where the vapour salinity tends to zero.
    • Densities, enthalpies and heat capacities of brines differ slightly, because they are evaluated through the water equation of state.
    • Any other backend name raises NotImplementedError.

    Model domain¶

    The correlations are published for 0–1000 °C, 1–5000 bar and X_NaCl = 0–1. The H₂O–NaCl class reports this domain as Tmin() = 273.16 K, Tmax() = 1273.15 K, pmin() = 1e5 Pa and pmax() = 5e8 Pa. Which inputs outside it are rejected is listed under Domain, input checks and errors.

    Verification¶

    What was compared¶

    • The author's published tables. The electronic supplements of both papers tabulate phase boundaries, coexisting compositions, densities and enthalpies. xThermo with IAPS-84 was compared with all ten tables: 29 680 values in 24 comparisons, one per tabulated quantity. 13 of the 24 agree to the printed precision. In the other 11, the differences at individual states are each traced to a cause: the deliberate differences described below, the other GEOMAR modifications, the defects at the saturation pressure of pure water, or table entries that are inconsistent with the published equations themselves (for example four liquid enthalpies that are exactly one tenth of the equation's value). Eight marginal rows, within 1.1 times the tolerance, remain without a diagnosed cause.
    • The published equations, evaluated independently. Wherever the papers give a single explicit formula, evaluating it directly with IAPS-84 reproduces the tables to their printed seven digits. This shows that the tables are a sound reference there.
    • The author's program SoWat 0.2 (2009), at selected states.
    • The water equations of state. IAPWS-95 reproduces the verification tables of the IAPWS release, the tables of Wagner & Pruß (2002) and an independent implementation, for density, enthalpy and density derivatives to round-off; its viscosity reproduces the check values of the IAPWS 2008 viscosity release. For IAPS-84, the publication (Kestin & Sengers 1986) tabulates check values only for pressure, isochoric heat capacity and the Helmholtz function: pressure and heat capacity are reproduced to the printed digits, the Helmholtz function up to the reference-state adjustment that the release allows.
    • Internal consistency. Heat capacities equal the temperature derivative of the enthalpy (to 1e-5), density derivatives are consistent with the density, and enthalpy-based input (H–p–X) returns the temperature of the corresponding T–p–X state. Exceptions are listed under Known limitations.

    The comparisons run as automated tests in the development repository; they are not part of the public source snapshot.

    What the agreement means¶

    Agreement with the published tables shows that xThermo implements the author's formulation as published. It is not an estimate of experimental accuracy. The accuracy of the model against experimental data is the one reported by its authors, for example an uncertainty of 0.1–0.2 % in density at low temperature and pressure, and an overestimate of the heat capacity by up to 10 % at high temperature and salinity (Driesner 2007). Disagreement with an individual table entry does not by itself prove an error in xThermo, because a few entries are inconsistent with the published equations.

    What has not been verified¶

    • the viscosity of brines (Klyukin et al. 2017) and the IAPS-84 water viscosity;
    • the properties of halite and of liquid NaCl;
    • density derivatives and heat capacities against experimental data;
    • the phase region away from the tabulated states (the tables lie almost entirely on phase boundaries);
    • IAPS-84 beyond its published check values;
    • results with the IAPWS-95 backend against the author's tables, which were computed with IAPS-84.

    Differences from the published formulation¶

    Other implementations of the same model include the author's SoWat program, his tabulated supplementary data, ProBrine and earlier GEOMAR MATLAB code. Three differences between them and xThermo are known and were decided after a scientific assessment. Questions about the author's undocumented treatments remain open; the decisions can be revisited if he clarifies them.

    Heat capacity¶

    • xThermo: c_P = (∂h/∂T)_{p,x}, the published Eq. 27 of Driesner (2007). It is consistent with the enthalpy.
    • SoWat 0.2 and ProBrine multiply Eq. 27 by 1/(1 + x), where x is the NaCl mole fraction of the phase, and leave the enthalpy unchanged. SoWat describes the factor as missed in the original publication; no derivation of it has been published.
    • Applied to c_P alone, the factor makes c_P inconsistent with the enthalpy. It is not a change of units or composition basis, and the authors' own comparison with experimental data does not indicate a need for a correction of this form. It has not been tested against experimental data here.
    • Consequence: SoWat and ProBrine heat capacities of saline phases are lower by the factor 1/(1 + x): about 1 % for seawater, 9 % at 26 wt% NaCl, 23 % at 58 wt% and up to 50 % for nearly pure NaCl liquid.

    Liquid enthalpy of hot brines at low pressure¶

    • This applies to the liquid at T ≥ 600 °C and p ≤ 390.147 bar. There, the published enthalpy correlation can map the brine onto a water state in the vapour field, where it is not usable.
    • xThermo uses a smooth extrapolation constructed at GEOMAR. It has the same form as the published density extrapolation for this region, is continuous at 390.147 bar, and its heat capacity is consistent with it. Elsewhere it reproduces the published equation.
    • It differs from the author's tabulated values by up to about 3 % (median 0.8 %), which corresponds to −19 to +28 K in the temperature of a given enthalpy. The author's treatment in this region is not described in the paper, and none of the forms tested reproduces it.
    • This is a difference between two implementations that both extrapolate beyond the fitted data. The physical accuracy of either in this region has not been established.

    Vapour composition above the melting point of NaCl¶

    • Above the triple point of NaCl (800.7 °C), the published vapour-composition equation (Driesner & Heinrich 2007, Eq. 15) contains a term that is not defined.
    • xThermo sets it to 1. This is consistent with the published condition that the boiling curve of NaCl bounds the V+L field from below.
    • The author's program uses an undocumented, temperature-dependent value instead. Against it, xThermo's vapour NaCl content is about 1.3 % lower at 810 °C, 6–13 % lower at 850–900 °C and up to about 24 % lower at 1000 °C (12 % at 1000 bar). The absolute amounts are small: below 300 bar the vapour carries 0.01–0.2 wt% NaCl.
    • Below 800.7 °C, xThermo reproduces the author's tabulated compositions.
    • Available experimental data do not decide between the two treatments.

    Other modifications¶

    These were introduced in the GEOMAR implementations from which xThermo was ported, or in the port itself. The sizes are differences from the published equations or the author's tables at the tested states.

    Modification Effect
    The low-temperature correction term D(T) of the density mapping (Driesner 2007, Eq. 13) is left out, because it can push the scaled temperature below the freezing point of water brine density lower by up to 0.18 % at 10–50 °C and 500–2000 bar near halite saturation; ≤ 0.01 % above about 50 °C
    Molar mass of water 18.015268 g/mol instead of the paper's 18.015 g/mol ≤ 1.5e-5 in density and in conversions between mass and mole fraction
    Low-pressure density extrapolation (Eq. 17) in a different working form; the printed equation is ambiguous brine density 0.16–0.46 % off at 1–15 bar near boiling, up to 3 % below 1 bar
    High-temperature, low-pressure density extrapolation (Eq. 18) with the region clarified by the author in 2018; working form not reproduced exactly ≤ 0.06 % for hot, salt-rich liquids
    Liquid branch of the V+L surface above 800.7 °C anchored at zero pressure instead of on the NaCl boiling curve ≤ 1e-5 in liquid composition
    The 900 °C upper bound of PROST's water viscosity is not applied above 900 °C the IAPS-84 viscosity is an extrapolation; it differs from the IAPWS 2008 formulation by −0.4 to +6.1 % at 900–1100 °C

    Multiphase properties¶

    Each phase has its own (intrinsic) properties: Rho_l, H_l, Cp_l, Mu_l, X_l for the liquid, _v for the vapour and _h for halite. They are computed from the published correlations at the phase's own composition. The bulk fields describe the mixture:

    Field Single phase Two or three phases
    S_l, S_v, S_h 1 for the present phase volume fractions of the phases (H₂O–NaCl). The pure-water classes in xthermo.H2O report the vapour mass fraction instead.
    Rho phase density Σ S_i ρ_i
    H phase enthalpy mass-weighted: Σ S_i ρ_i h_i / ρ
    Cp (∂h/∂T)_{p,x} of the phase frozen heat capacity: Σ S_i ρ_i c_P,i / ρ, at fixed phase amounts and compositions; 0 in V+L+H
    IsothermalCompressibility of the phase equilibrium compressibility in V+L, V+H and L+H: from the bulk density at fixed T and bulk composition, with the phases re-equilibrating; 0 in V+L+H
    IsobaricExpansivity, dRhodT, dRhodP of the phase not computed (0); use the phase fields *_l, *_v
    Mu phase viscosity not defined: a placeholder value of 1 in V+L, V+H and L+H. Two enthalpy-based paths set S_l μ_l + S_v μ_v instead: V+L+H, and V+L of pure water. Use Mu_l and Mu_v.

    Frozen and equilibrium heat capacity. In a multiphase state the enthalpy of the mixture also changes because mass moves between phases of different enthalpy (latent heat) and because the coexisting compositions change with temperature. The equilibrium heat capacity (∂H/∂T)_{p,X} contains these terms; the bulk Cp does not. Near phase boundaries the difference is large. In V+L at 400 °C, 250 bar and 10 wt% NaCl, Cp is 9.2 kJ/(kg K), whereas the temperature derivative of the bulk enthalpy is 31 kJ/(kg K):

    import numpy as np
import xthermo
from xthermo import H2ONaCl

sw = H2ONaCl.cH2ONaCl("IAPS84")
dT = 0.01                                            # K
s = xthermo.tpx(sw, 673.15 + np.array([-dT, 0.0, dT]), 250e5, 0.10)
print(s.phase_name[1])                               # V+L
print(s.Cp[1] / 1e3)                                 # frozen: 9.2 kJ/(kg K)
print((s.H[2] - s.H[0]) / (2 * dT) / 1e3)            # equilibrium (dH/dT): 31 kJ/(kg K)

    How bulk heat capacity and bulk viscosity of multiphase states should be defined is an open question; the effective properties of a multiphase flow (viscosity, relative permeability, storage terms) belong to the flow model. The fields above may change when it is decided.

    Saturation and phase-amount conventions. The H₂O–NaCl saturations are volume fractions. The pure-water classes report the vapour mass fraction (quality) in S_v; convert with w_v = S_v ρ_v / ρ when you combine the two. The pure-water classes leave the bulk two-phase Cp at 0.

    Absent phases. Fields of a phase that is not present keep 0 in the C++ structure and the classic interface; xthermo.tpx and xthermo.hpx can return NaN for them instead (absent="nan"; see Evaluating states).

    Domain, input checks and errors¶

    Only part of the model domain is enforced at run time:

    Input Behaviour
    T above 1273.15 K, or H above the enthalpy at 1273.15 K (H₂O–NaCl) OutOfRangeError
    non-finite T, p, H or X; p ≤ 0; X < 0 InvalidInputError
    p < 1 bar not checked: values are extrapolations of the correlations
    p > 5000 bar "IAPS84": not checked, values are returned; "IAPWS95": OutOfRangeError from the water backend
    X > 1 not checked: values are returned (X = 1.2 gives an "L+H" state), but they have no physical meaning

    Results outside the published domain have not been validated. Check your inputs before calling the library.

    When an evaluation fails, the library raises a typed error rather than returning a previous or partial state. In C++ the errors derive from xThermal::xThermalBaseError. In Python they are subclasses of xthermo.EvaluationError: InvalidInputError (bad input), OutOfRangeError (outside the range the model or backend can evaluate), NotConvergedError (a numerical solution failed) and NotAvailableError (not provided for this model or state). The C++ functions tryUpdateState_TPX and tryUpdateState_HPX return an EvaluationStatus instead of throwing.

    Array evaluation. xthermo.tpx and xthermo.hpx raise the first error by default (errors="raise"). With errors="nan" they evaluate every point, set the failed points to NaN and report each point's outcome in a Status array, so that valid points are kept. See Errors and status.

    Individual properties. A property that cannot be evaluated at an otherwise valid state is NaN, while the other properties remain valid: for example the IAPS-84 water viscosity outside its validity range (above 5000 bar, above 3500 bar at temperatures over 150 °C, and above 3000 bar over 600 °C), and brine viscosities that depend on it.

    Phase-boundary functions. Some helpers do not raise outside their own temperature range; they print a warning and return 0 (for example X_Critical for temperatures outside the critical curve).

    Known limitations¶

    Limitations of the published correlations¶

    • Extrapolated regions. Densities of brines below about 15 bar near boiling, and densities and liquid enthalpies above about 600 °C below 390 bar, come from extrapolations (see above). Pressures below 1 bar are outside the model.
    • Negative compressibility of some saline liquids near 1 bar. The published density correlation gives a negative isothermal compressibility for stable, single-phase or halite-saturated liquids at about ≤ 5 bar, 10–100 °C and salinities above about three quarters of halite saturation (for example −3e-5 per bar at 50 °C and 1 bar, near halite saturation). A stable phase requires a positive compressibility, so the correlation is not thermodynamically consistent in this corner. The effect on density is about 2e-5 relative, within the stated uncertainty of the correlation. xThermo keeps the published correlation unchanged; its derivatives are consistent with the density it returns.
    • Near the critical point of water the IAPS-84 formulation is excluded from its published range of validity, and both water formulations are less certain. Dilute brines inherit this through the scaled temperature.
    • Vapour composition above 800.7 °C and the liquid enthalpy of hot brines at low pressure are uncertain, as described above.

    Confirmed numerical defects¶

    These are known, reproducible and open. Their effects are small or local; workarounds are given where they exist.

    • Section geometry (Slice_constP, Slice_constT, and the plotting functions that use them). Each state is computed correctly by tpx and hpx; only the drawn regions are affected.
      • In isobaric enthalpy–salinity sections below about 221 bar, a thin sliver of the V+L region at salinities below 0.13 wt% NaCl is missing.
      • In isothermal sections, the liquid + halite region continues below the three-phase pressure, where vapour + halite is stable. The plotting functions draw vapour + halite on top, so their figures are correct.
      • Isothermal sections below 374 °C contain a spurious "critical point" at X = 0.
    • Pure water exactly at its saturation pressure (within about one part in a million).
      • With IAPS-84, the phase label and the density can belong to different phases (at 150 °C, a state labelled "Vapor" with the liquid density), and states inside the backend's two-phase tolerance are evaluated 0.1 K away from the requested temperature. Vapour densities step by up to 0.37 % there.
      • With IAPWS-95 the evaluation fails with NotConvergedError.
    • IAPWS-95 brines at low temperature and at 5000 bar. With the IAPWS-95 backend, brines below about 7 °C (10 wt% NaCl) to about 21 °C (50 wt%) cannot be evaluated, because the scaled temperature falls below the range of the water formulation (OutOfRangeError). At exactly 5000 bar, brines of 30 wt% NaCl and more fail below 129 °C (30 wt%) to 238 °C (50 wt% and more) as well, while 4999.99 bar works. IAPS-84 evaluates both.
    • Density derivatives in the low-pressure extrapolation. At 1–5 bar near boiling, ∂ρ/∂p of brines is off by up to −46 % and has the wrong sign at 70 °C and 1 bar; ∂ρ/∂T is within 0.1 %. The density itself is unaffected.
    • Density noise with IAPWS-95 at high temperature and low pressure. In the high-temperature extrapolation, brine densities with IAPWS-95 show small jumps of about 6e-4 relative, and ∂ρ/∂T is rough to about 1e-3. IAPS-84 is not affected.
    • Liquid enthalpy below 1 bar. The extrapolation used when the scaled temperature of the liquid enthalpy falls into the vapour field of water uses a density where an enthalpy is intended. Inside 1–5000 bar it is not reached for liquids that exist; it gives wrong values below 1 bar (up to +250 % at 30–60 °C) and for direct phase-property calls at states where no liquid exists.

    Under investigation¶

    These are reproducible observations whose cause or correct treatment has not been established. Both concern pure NaCl (X = 1) near its melting point.

    • Enthalpy-based input inside the melting range. For pure NaCl, enthalpies between those of halite and of the liquid at the melting temperature correspond to halite and liquid coexisting. hpx returns such a state as successful, but with a different enthalpy: at 250 bar, an input of 1.031 MJ/kg gives an L+H state at 806.9 °C with H = 0.807 MJ/kg, 22 % lower. Elsewhere, the returned enthalpy equals the input to within 1e-6. Do not rely on hpx for pure NaCl inside its melting range.
    • Enthalpy of liquid NaCl and halite. At the melting point the enthalpy of pure NaCl rises from the halite value to the liquid value, by 0.25 MJ/kg at 1 bar (0.79 to 1.04 MJ/kg). Liquid NaCl is computed with the brine correlations at X = 1, halite with its own correlation; the halite enthalpy's reference value has not been traced to a published source. Whether the two enthalpies share a reference state, and whether the step agrees with the heat of fusion of the published formulation, has not been checked. The properties of pure NaCl have not been independently verified.
