    Model scope and limitations

    xThermo implements the H₂O–NaCl correlations of Driesner & Heinrich (2007) and Driesner (2007), combined with an equation of state for pure water. This page states what the implementation computes, where it differs from other implementations of the same model, and how it behaves outside the model domain.

    Model domain and runtime checks

    The correlations are published for 0–1000 °C, 1–5000 bar and X_NaCl = 0–1. The H₂O–NaCl class reports this domain as Tmin() = 273.16 K, Tmax() = 1273.15 K, pmin() = 1e5 Pa and pmax() = 5e8 Pa.

    Only part of the domain is enforced at run time:

    Input Behaviour
    T > 1273.15 K error (UpdateState_TPX, findPhaseRegion_TPX)
    H above the enthalpy at 1273.15 K error (UpdateState_HPX)
    p < 1 bar not checked: values are returned
    p > 5000 bar "IAPS84": not checked, values are returned; "IAPWS95": error from the water backend
    X > 1 not checked: values are returned (e.g. X = 1.2 gives an "L+H" state)
    X < 0 error from the water backend

    Results outside the published domain are extrapolations of the correlations and have not been validated. Check your inputs before calling the library.

    Errors

    • C++: errors are thrown as exceptions derived from xThermal::xThermalBaseError (Exception.h), for example OutOfRangeError, ValueError and NotImplementedError.
    • Python: these appear as built-in exceptions:
    C++ error Python exception
    out of range, invalid value ValueError
    not implemented (e.g. an unknown backend name) NotImplementedError
    solver failure, other errors RuntimeError
    • Array calls: a call with NumPy arrays stops at the first point that fails and returns no partial result. To keep the valid points, evaluate point by point and catch the exception.
    • Helper functions: some phase-boundary helpers do not raise outside their own temperature range. They print a warning and return 0 (for example X_Critical for T outside the critical-curve range).

    Water backends

    Backend Formulation Range of the pure-water class
    "IAPS84" (default of the command-line tool) IAPS-84 (Haar, Gallagher & Kell 1984), computed with PROST 4.1 260–2500 K, up to 30 000 bar
    "IAPWS95" IAPWS-95 (Wagner & Pruß 2002) 273.165–2273.15 K, up to 5000 bar

    Notes: - The Driesner correlations were fitted with IAPS-84. "IAPS84" is therefore the reference backend. - Most phase-boundary correlations do not depend on the backend: for example the V+L+H pressure, and the critical curve and V+L compositions above 500 °C. - Below 500 °C, the critical pressure with "IAPWS95" is up to about 0.07 % higher than with "IAPS84". Its critical curve has a step of about 0.03 % at 500 °C. - Below 500 °C, V+L vapour compositions differ between the backends by up to about 2.5 %. - Densities, enthalpies and heat capacities of the brine differ slightly between the backends, because they are evaluated through the water equation of state. - Any other backend name raises NotImplementedError.

    Phase properties and bulk properties

    Each phase has its own properties: Rho_l, H_l, Cp_l, Mu_l, X_l for the liquid; _v for the vapour; _h for halite. They are computed from the published correlations.

    The bulk fields are defined as follows:

    Field Single phase Two or three phases
    S_l, S_v, S_h 1 for the present phase volume fractions of the phases (H₂O–NaCl). The pure-water classes in xThermal.H2O report mass fractions instead.
    Rho phase density Σ S_i ρ_i
    H phase enthalpy mass-weighted: Σ S_i ρ_i h_i / ρ
    Cp (∂h/∂T)_{P,X} of the phase (Driesner 2007, Eq. 27) mass-weighted phase heat capacities, Σ S_i ρ_i c_P,i / ρ, at fixed phase amounts and compositions. Contains no latent heat and is not the derivative of the bulk enthalpy. Example: in V+L at 400 °C, 250 bar, 10 wt % it is 9.2 kJ/(kg K), whereas (∂H/∂T)_{P,X} of the bulk is 31 kJ/(kg K). In V+L+H, Cp is 0.
    Mu phase viscosity not defined. Mu is a placeholder value of 1 in V+L, L+H and V+H. Only two H–P–X branches set S_l μ_l + S_v μ_v: V+L+H, and the low-salinity / low-pressure V+L fallback. The same V+L state can therefore report 1 or a weighted value, depending on the call. Use Mu_l and Mu_v.
    dRhodT, dRhodP, IsobaricExpansivity, IsothermalCompressibility phase derivatives not defined as bulk quantities (the first three are 0); use the phase fields *_l, *_v

    How bulk heat capacity and bulk viscosity of multiphase states should be defined is an open question. The fields above may change when it is decided.

    Differences from other implementations of the model

    Other implementations of the same model include the author's SoWat program, his tabulated supplementary data, ProBrine and earlier GEOMAR MATLAB code. Three differences between them and xThermo are known and deliberate.

    Heat capacity. - xThermo: c_P = (∂h/∂T)_{P,X}, the published Eq. 27. It is consistent with the enthalpy. - SoWat 0.2 and ProBrine multiply Eq. 27 by 1/(1 + x), where x is the NaCl mole fraction of the phase. They do not change the enthalpy. - This factor is not part of the published formulation, and its justification has not been documented. Applied to c_P alone, it makes c_P inconsistent with the enthalpy, so xThermo does not apply it. - Consequence: SoWat and ProBrine heat capacities of saline phases are lower by 1/(1 + x). That is about 1 % for seawater, 9 % at 26 wt % and up to 50 % for nearly pure NaCl liquid.

    Liquid enthalpy above 600 °C and below about 390 bar. - This applies to the liquid phase at T ≥ 600 °C and p ≤ 390.15 bar. There, the published enthalpy correlation can map the brine onto a water state in the vapour field, where it is not usable. - xThermo uses a smooth extrapolation adopted from earlier GEOMAR code. It has the same form as the published density extrapolation, and its heat capacity is consistent with it. - It differs from the author's tabulated values by up to about 3 %, which corresponds to −19 to +28 K in the temperature of a given enthalpy. - This is a difference between two implementations. The physical accuracy in this region has not been established.

    Vapour composition in V+L above 800.7 °C. - Above the triple point of NaCl, the published vapour-composition equation (Driesner & Heinrich 2007, Eq. 15) contains a term that is not defined. - xThermo sets it to 1. This is consistent with the published condition that the NaCl boiling curve bounds the V+L field from below. - The author's program uses an undocumented temperature-dependent value instead. Against it, xThermo's vapour NaCl content is about 1.3 % lower at 810 °C and up to about 24 % lower at 1000 °C. - Below 800.7 °C, xThermo reproduces the author's tabulated compositions. - Available experimental data do not decide between the two treatments.
